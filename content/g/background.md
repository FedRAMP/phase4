# Background on the A-130 Class G Pilot

When a federal agency operates a cloud service for other agencies, it already has an authorizing official responsible for accepting the risks of operating that service. OMB Circular A-130 and the Federal Information Processing Standards and NIST publications that support it govern how the agency secures, assesses, authorizes, and monitors the system. Offering the service to other agencies also brings it within the scope of FedRAMP under OMB Memorandum M-24-15.

The Agency-Provided Offerings (APO) ruleset establishes a new FedRAMP Certification pathway specifically for agency-provided cloud services that already meet government information security regulations. This ruleset exempts agency-provided cloud services from FedRAMP rules that are designed for the assessment and assurance of commercial cloud services whose operators are not subject to the rules and regulations that apply to agencies.

Under this pathway, FedRAMP relies on the provider agency’s authorization and ongoing oversight. A customer agency can use the provider’s authorization materials to decide whether the service is appropriate for its own use. Each customer agency remains responsible for authorizing the systems in which it uses the service.

## From Offerings By Government to Class G

The ruleset completed initial FedRAMP Board review in June 2026. FedRAMP published it for public comment in August under the name *Offerings By Government*. The proposal mapped an agency’s authorization to an existing FedRAMP Certification Class based on impact level. For example, an offering with a Moderate impact authorization could receive a Class C Certification. The comment period closed in September. [RFC-0032 Offerings By Government](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0032/)

On November 16, 2026, FedRAMP announced a single Certification Profile for agency-provided offerings. Its Certification Type is A-130, its Class is G, and its path is Agency Certification. The agency authorization’s FIPS-200 impact level (Low, Moderate, or High) is recorded separately. All Class G offerings follow the same FedRAMP rules. Each agency provider implements the control baseline appropriate to its offering’s impact level. [NTC-0019 Outcome of RFC-0032 and Class G Pilot Announcement](https://www.fedramp.gov/notices/0019/)

## Agency-Provided Offerings

To be eligible for a FedRAMP Class G Certification, agency-provided cloud service offerings must meet all of the following requirements:

1. The cloud service offering is within the scope of FedRAMP (especially, it is a shared service intended to be used by multiple agencies).
2. The cloud service offering is operated by an agency (including under direct contract where legally relevant).
3. The authorizing official, system owner, privacy official, and all other key RMF roles are filled by appropriate federal officials or contractors operating under authority and responsibility delegated from federal officials.

System owners who are unsure whether their offering meets these requirements should consult with appropriate counsel.
